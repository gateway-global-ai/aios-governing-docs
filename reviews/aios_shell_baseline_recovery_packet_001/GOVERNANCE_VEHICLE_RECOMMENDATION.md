# Governance vehicle recommendation

## Current positioning

The correct current vehicle is:

- a **supporting packet**
- plus its **review packet**

It is **not** yet a shell-baseline amendment and it is **not** yet an addendum under review for ratification.

## Why

The packet is extracting and evaluating current implementation truth.

That is evidence-gathering and planning support work, not binding doctrine creation. The review result for Round 1 also shows the packet itself still needs revision before it can serve as the foundation approval gate.

The final seven-review advisory panel further confirms that taxonomy and authority are part of the foundation problem itself, not merely later cleanup tasks.

## When a doctrine vehicle becomes appropriate

Open a narrow doctrine vehicle only if the revised and human-reviewed packet still shows that the missing baseline behaviors cannot be resolved through:

- implementation correction
- specification alignment
- clearer use of already-ratified doctrine
- packet refinement

## Narrow doctrine scope only

If a doctrine vehicle is needed, keep it limited to shell-baseline foundation concerns:

- shell baseline control surfaces
- entry and user-state classification
- shell state separation of powers
- minimum baseline screen artifacts
- the governed rule that broader work cannot advance until shell-baseline approval exists in the correct binding vehicle

## Explicit non-scope

Do not combine this future vehicle with:

- broader tenant artifacts
- marketplace or commercial expansion
- rich document-system expansion beyond the baseline foundation
- unrelated interface redesign

## Recommended sequence

1. Revise the supporting packet.
2. Rerun review.
3. Submit the revised packet for human approval.
4. Only then decide whether a narrow shell-baseline addendum or amendment is actually required.

## Amendment-planning target set

If a future doctrine vehicle becomes necessary, it should begin from the concern set captured in `AMENDMENT_PLAN_UPDATE.md`, with taxonomy treated as a first-order target alongside authority, baseline capability grounding, and the missing identity/profile/document foundations.
